## What this means in practice

- **You may** fork the code, modify it, redistribute it, and even sell a product
  built on it — that is what MIT allows.
- **You may not** call your fork, distribution, or service "BlackEclipse", use the

- **You may** say, truthfully: "built on BlackEclipse", "a fork of BlackEclipse",

  is clear you are not the official project.
- **You may not** imply endorsement, sponsorship, or official status, or register

## Forks

A fork must use a different name and logo and must state plainly that it is not
the official BlackEclipse project. The MIT copyright notice must be preserved.

## Why this exists

MIT gives your code away. Without a trademark notice, anyone could take the code,
keep the famous name, and capture the users you worked to attract. The code is
free; the identity is not. This is how Firefox, PostgreSQL, and Signal operate.
