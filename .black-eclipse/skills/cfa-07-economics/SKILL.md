# CFA Level I — Economics

> **Not affiliated with CFA Institute.** These are original study notes, not CFA Institute materials. "CFA" is a trademark of CFA Institute. See `../CFA-NOTICE.md`.

**Weight 6-9% · 8 modules · seventh in the study order.** Descriptive and
conceptual for the most part, with two calculation pockets (profit maximisation
and FX cross-rates). Lower priority than the 11-14% topics but cheap marks.

## Learning modules (official 2027)

| # | Module | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Firm and Market Structures | **Breakeven and shutdown points**; economies and diseconomies of scale; perfect competition, monopolistic competition, oligopoly, pure monopoly; optimal price and output; concentration measures and their limits |
| 2 | Understanding Business Cycles | Business cycle phases; **credit cycles**; resource use, consumer and business activity, housing and external trade across the cycle; economic indicators |
| 3 | Fiscal Policy | Monetary vs fiscal policy; roles and objectives; whether national debt relative to GDP matters; tools and their advantages/disadvantages; implementation difficulties; expansionary vs contractionary |
| 4 | Monetary Policy | Central bank roles and objectives; **monetary policy tools and the transmission mechanism**; links to growth, inflation, interest and exchange rates; inflation/interest rate/exchange rate targeting; limitations; **interaction of monetary and fiscal policy** |
| 5 | **Introduction to Geopolitics** | Cooperation vs competition; geopolitics and globalization; the World Bank, IMF and WTO; **geopolitical risk**; tools of geopolitics and their economic impact; impact on investments |
| 6 | International Trade | Benefits and costs of trade; **tariffs, quotas and export subsidies** and their implications; trading blocs, common markets, economic unions |
| 7 | Capital Flows and the FX Market | The FX market, functions and participants; **nominal vs real exchange rates**; percentage change in a currency; **exchange rate regimes**; effects on trade and capital flows; capital restrictions |
| 8 | Exchange Rate Calculations | **Currency cross-rates**; the arbitrage relationship between spot and forward rates and interest rates; **forward rate from points or percentage terms**; forward discount or premium |

**Note:** module 5 (geopolitics) is a newer module — a pre-2024 course will not
have it.

## What to learn first

1. **Module 1** (market structures) — includes the only real micro calculation
   (breakeven/shutdown).
2. **Modules 3-4** (fiscal and monetary policy) — the highest-yield descriptive
   block.
3. **Modules 7-8** (FX) — the calculation module; cross-rates and forward points.
4. **Modules 2, 5 and 6** — read for recognition.

## Tier 1 — must memorise

- **Breakeven quantity** = fixed costs / (price - variable cost per unit); the
  **shutdown point** is where price falls below average variable cost (short run)
  vs all costs (long run).
- **Cross-rate computation** via a common currency, including bid/ask handling.
- **Forward rate from spot and interest rates** (covered interest parity), and
  **forward points** convention.
- **Real exchange rate** = nominal rate adjusted for the price levels of the two
  countries.
- **Percentage change in a currency pair** and the convention (base vs price
  currency).
- **Money multiplier / required reserve** relationship if examined.

## Tier 2 — must understand

- The four market structures and how price, output and entry differ across them.
- Why an oligopoly needs game-theoretic reasoning rather than a single price rule.
- The monetary transmission mechanism: how a policy rate reaches output and
  inflation.
- Why a central bank cannot target the exchange rate and the interest rate
  independently.
- How geopolitical risk transmits to asset prices (supply shocks, sanctions,
  capital controls).
- Why trading blocs raise trade within and can divert it from outside.

## Common traps

- **Breakeven vs shutdown** — the shutdown rule uses average *variable* cost in the
  short run.
- **Base vs price currency** in a quote: "USD/EUR" is not the same as "EUR/USD".
- Getting the **cross-rate direction backwards** by inverting the wrong leg.
- Applying **covered interest parity in the wrong direction** to derive the
  forward rate.
- Assuming a **tariff and a quota** have identical economic effects (they differ in
  who captures the rent).
- Confusing **fiscal and monetary policy tools** — one is government spending and
  taxation, the other is central bank operations.
- Treating **nominal and real exchange rates** as interchangeable.
- Forgetting that **expansionary fiscal policy can crowd out** private investment.

## Typical question patterns

- Compute breakeven quantity, then decide whether to shut down in the short run.
- Compute a cross-rate from two quoted pairs.
- Derive a forward rate from spot rates and interest rates, then state whether the
  forward is at a premium or discount.
- Identify whether a described policy is fiscal or monetary, expansionary or
  contractionary.
- Classify a market structure from its described characteristics.
- An item on how a geopolitical event affects a described investment.
