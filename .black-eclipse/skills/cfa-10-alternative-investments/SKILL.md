# CFA Level I — Alternative Investments

**Weight 6-9% (was 7-10%) · 7 modules · tenth in the study order.** The most
marks-per-hour topic on the exam: descriptive, distinctive, and only seven
modules. It also carries the digital-assets module that older material lacks.

## Learning modules (official 2027)

| # | Module | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Alternative Investment Features, Methods, and Structures | Categories of alternatives; **direct vs co-investment vs fund investment**; ownership and compensation structures |
| 2 | Alternative Investment Performance and Returns | Performance appraisal; **calculate returns before and after fees** |
| 3 | Investments in Private Capital: Equity and Debt | Private equity and private debt features; **diversification benefits** |
| 4 | Real Estate and Infrastructure | Features and investment characteristics of each |
| 5 | Natural Resources | Raw land, timberland, farmland; commodities; sources of risk, return and diversification |
| 6 | Hedge Funds | Investment features vs other asset classes; forms and vehicles; sources of risk, return and diversification |
| 7 | **Introduction to Digital Assets** | **Distributed ledger technology** applications; investment features contrasted with other asset classes; forms and vehicles; sources of risk, return and diversification |

## What to learn first

1. **Module 1** (features, methods, structures) — the vocabulary everything else
   uses.
2. **Module 2** (returns and fees) — the only real calculation in the topic.
3. **Modules 3-6** — learn as a comparison table across asset classes, not as
   separate subjects.
4. **Module 7** (digital assets) — descriptive; learn applications, features,
   forms, risks.

## Tier 1 — must memorise

- **Net-of-fee return** with a **management fee** on assets and an **incentive
  fee** — and critically, **whether the incentive fee is charged on the gross
  return or on the return net of the management fee**. The question will state
  which; the answer changes.
- **Direct vs fund investment** trade-offs: control and fees versus diversification
  and liquidity.
- The **diversification argument**: alternatives help when their correlation with
  the portfolio is low, but **smoothed or appraisal-based pricing understates
  reported volatility and correlation**.
- **Liquidity characteristics** per asset class (lockups, gates, notice periods
  for hedge funds; illiquidity for private capital and real estate).
- **Distributed ledger technology applications** in finance, and the digital-asset
  risk categories.

## Tier 2 — must understand

- Why reported hedge fund and real-estate returns can overstate risk-adjusted
  performance (stale pricing, survivorship, backfill bias).
- How private capital compensation structures align or misalign manager and
  investor interests.
- Why infrastructure is treated as a distinct class (regulated, long-lived cash
  flows).
- How digital assets differ from traditional classes on custody, settlement and
  valuation (the curriculum wants characteristics, not a valuation model).

## Common traps

- **Computing the incentive fee on the wrong base** — the single most common error
  in this topic.
- Forgetting the **management fee is on assets**, not on returns.
- Assuming alternatives deliver their **stated diversification benefit during
  stress** — correlations rise in drawdowns, and stale pricing hides it.
- Attributing **equity-like liquidity** to private capital or real estate.
- Treating **commodities as an income-producing asset** — they produce no cash
  flow.
- Expecting a **digital-asset valuation formula** — Level I asks for
  characteristics and risks only.
- Assuming a **hedge fund lockup and a gate are the same** — one delays exit, the
  other restricts it.

## Typical question patterns

- Compute a net-of-fee return, then recompute it with the other fee base and
  compare.
- Identify the vehicle (direct, co-investment, fund) that suits a described
  investor.
- Compare two alternative classes on liquidity, correlation or compensation.
- Classify a described risk or characteristic to the right asset class.
- An item on DLT applications or digital-asset risks.

## Practice target and revisits

- **100-140 questions** — the lowest volume of any topic, which makes this the
  best value on the exam.
- Revisit once in the revision phase; the material retains well because it is
  distinctive.

## Dependencies

- Needs: the correlation/diversification concept from Portfolio Construction.
- Feeds: nothing downstream (it is Level II and III where alternatives deepen).

## Failure handling

- A return question that does not state the incentive-fee base -> say the base is
  ambiguous and give both results rather than silently choosing one.
