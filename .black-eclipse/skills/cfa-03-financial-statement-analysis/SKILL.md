# CFA Level I — Financial Statement Analysis

**Weight 11-14% · 12 modules · third topic in the study order.** FSA is the
accounting core. It is the topic candidates most often underestimate, and it
feeds Equities multiples, Fixed Income credit analysis and Corporate Finance.

## Critical exam rule

**Questions assume IFRS unless the question explicitly states U.S. GAAP.** When a
question names GAAP, the differences are the point being tested. Never answer a
GAAP question with the IFRS treatment out of habit.

## What to learn first

1. **Modules 1-5** (the framework and the three statements) — everything else
   builds on cash flow and accrual mechanics.
2. **Modules 6-9** (inventories, long-term assets, liabilities, taxes) — the
   accounting-choice modules, where the exam lives.
3. **Modules 10-11** (reporting quality, ratios, DuPont) — the analytical payoff
   and the most reliably examined module.
4. **Module 12** (modelling) — read for recognition, not deep computation.

## Tier 1 — must memorise

- **Basic EPS** = (net income - preferred dividends) / weighted average shares;
  **Diluted EPS** with the **if-converted** method and the **treasury stock**
  method, and the **antidilution test**.
- **Current ratio**, **quick ratio**, **cash ratio**.
- **Inventory turnover** and **days of inventory on hand**; **receivables
  turnover** and **days sales outstanding**; **payables turnover** and days.
- **Gross, operating and net profit margins.**
- **Debt-to-equity**, **financial leverage**, **interest coverage**.
- **ROE, ROA**, and **five-way DuPont:** ROE = net profit margin x asset turnover
  x financial leverage (and the extended version splitting tax burden and
  interest burden).
- **FCFF** and **FCFE** from net income and from cash flow from operations.
- **FIFO vs LIFO** effects on inventory, cost of goods sold, and net income when
  prices are rising (the single most examined inventory item).
- **Deferred tax:** why a DTL arises (book income > taxable income), why a DTA
  arises (the reverse, or a carryforward).

## Tier 2 — must understand

- Why a cash flow statement cannot be manipulated as easily as accrual earnings.
- The indirect-to-direct conversion mechanics (not just the definition).
- Leases: how a lessee's ratios change, and what the operating/finance lease
  distinction does to the balance sheet.
- Impairment: what reverses under IFRS and what does not, and which ratios move.
- Conservative vs aggressive accounting, and how each flatters or penalises
  reported figures.
- The difference between reporting quality and earnings quality.

## Common traps

- **Answering a US GAAP question with the IFRS treatment** (or the reverse).
- **FIFO/LIFO direction errors** under rising prices — get the direction of the
  inventory, COGS and income effects right before computing anything.
- Forgetting to **subtract preferred dividends** before basic EPS.
- **Skipping the antidilution test** — a security that would raise EPS must be
  excluded from diluted EPS.
- Using **ending instead of average** balance sheet values in ratio formulas that
  call for averages.
- Treating **interest paid** as an operating cash flow under both standards (IFRS
  allows classification flexibility; US GAAP puts interest paid in operating).
- Confusing **statutory, effective and cash tax rates**.
- Reading a **non-GAAP measure** as if it were a reported figure.
- Forgetting that a **stock dividend or split** restates the weighted average
  share count retroactively for the whole year.

## Typical question patterns

- Compute diluted EPS given a convertible or an option series.
- Adjust ratios after a change of inventory method and state the direction.
- Trace a deferred tax balance to its cause and classify it.
- Compute ROE by DuPont and attribute a change to the right component.
- Identify the warning sign of low-quality reporting in a scenario.
- Compute FCFF/FCFE from a partial cash flow statement.

## Dependencies

- Needs: arithmetic and the TVM basics.
- Feeds: Equities multiples and forecasting; Fixed Income credit ratios; Corporate
  Finance (cash conversion cycle, ROIC).

## Failure handling

- A question that does not state the accounting standard -> assume IFRS and say so;
  if the answer turns on a GAAP difference, flag the ambiguity.
