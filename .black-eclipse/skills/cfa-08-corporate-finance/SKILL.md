# CFA Level I — Corporate Finance

**Weight 6-9% · 7 modules · eighth in the study order.** Renamed from *Corporate
Issuers* in 2027. Conceptual with one calculation cluster (NPV/IRR/ROIC, WACC).
The governance and stakeholder material is examined as much as the maths.

## Learning modules (official 2027)

| # | Module | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Organizational Forms, Corporate Issuer Features, and Ownership | Compare organisational forms; features of corporate issuers; public vs private ownership |
| 2 | Investors and Other Stakeholders | **Financial claims and motivations of lenders vs shareholders**; stakeholder groups and their interests; **ESG factors** investors consider |
| 3 | Corporate Governance: Conflicts, Mechanisms, Risks, and Benefits | The **principal-agent relationship** and conflicts; governance mechanisms; risks of poor governance and benefits of effective governance |
| 4 | Working Capital and Liquidity | The **cash conversion cycle**; liquidity levels; objectives and methods for managing working capital |
| 5 | Capital Investments and Capital Allocation | Types of capital investment; **NPV, IRR and ROIC**, and how they differ; capital allocation principles and pitfalls; **real options** |
| 6 | Capital Structure | **WACC calculation**; factors affecting capital structure and WACC; the **Modigliani-Miller propositions**; optimal and target capital structures |
| 7 | Business Models | Key features and types of business models |

## What to learn first

1. **Modules 1-3** (forms, stakeholders, governance) — descriptive vocabulary that
   the rest of the topic assumes.
2. **Module 4** (working capital) — the cash conversion cycle is a reliable item
   and overlaps FSA ratio work.
3. **Modules 5-6** (capital allocation, capital structure) — the calculation core,
   and the highest-value modules.
4. **Module 7** (business models) — read for recognition.

## Tier 1 — must memorise

- **NPV** = present value of cash flows minus the initial outlay; accept if
  positive.
- **IRR** = the discount rate at which NPV is zero; its pitfalls (multiple IRRs,
  scale, reinvestment assumption).
- **ROIC** = net operating profit after tax / invested capital.
- **WACC** = (weight of debt x after-tax cost of debt) + (weight of equity x cost
  of equity). The **after-tax** cost of debt is the examinable detail.
- **Cash conversion cycle** = days of inventory on hand + days sales outstanding -
  days payable outstanding.
- **Cost of equity** from CAPM (shared with Equities and Portfolio Construction).
- **Modigliani-Miller** propositions without taxes, and with taxes (**the tax
  shield raises firm value as leverage rises**).

## Tier 2 — must understand

- Why NPV is preferred to IRR on mutually exclusive projects of different scale.
- How ROIC compares with WACC as a value-creation test (ROIC > WACC creates
  value).
- What a real option adds that NPV ignores (the value of flexibility/deferral).
- The principal-agent problem and which governance mechanism addresses which
  conflict.
- Lender vs shareholder claims: why their interests diverge on risk-taking.
- Why ESG factors affect the cost of capital from an investor's view.

## Common traps

- Using **pre-tax cost of debt** in WACC — it is after-tax.
- Using **book weights** when the question specifies market weights (or the
  reverse).
- **IRR vs NPV ranking conflict** on mutually exclusive projects — NPV governs.
- Forgetting that **WACC is not the hurdle rate** for a project in a different risk
  class.
- Reversing the **sign convention on the cash conversion cycle** (it subtracts
  payables).
- Treating **dividends as an interest-like obligation** — they are discretionary.
- Assuming the **MM propositions hold with taxes** — they do not; the tax shield
  changes the conclusion.
- Confusing **governance mechanisms** (board independence, audit, compensation
  design) with outcomes.

## Typical question patterns

- Compute NPV and IRR for a project and state the decision, including a conflict
  case.
- Compute WACC from given component costs and weights.
- Compute the cash conversion cycle and interpret a change.
- Classify a described practice as addressing a specific governance conflict.
- An item on lender vs shareholder motivation in a distressed scenario.
- Identify which of two projects creates value using ROIC vs WACC.

  equity.

## Failure handling

  missing rather than assuming market value weights.
